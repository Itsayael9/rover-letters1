## Story — Surveyor 3 tells the two-machine tale

### `s3-story-01-meet.mp3` (~30 s)

```text
Hi! I'm Surveyor 3. I'm a robot lander, about as tall as a person. NASA set me down on the Moon on April 20, 1967, in a dark plain called Oceanus Procellarum. I don't have wheels. Three landing legs and round footpads hold me up in the dust. My solar panel is my lunch. It turns sunlight into power for my camera and radio. Now make a guess before I tell you the rest.
```

### `s3-story-02-job-a.mp3` (~30 s)

```text
Before people could land, NASA had one big worry: would a heavy ship sink into the Moon dust? My footpads pressed into the ground. If they sank, astronauts could not come. My blue scoop is a little shovel. It digs a trench and feels how hard the soil pushes back. My TV camera is my eye. It sends pictures of the crater all the way to Earth.
```

### `s3-story-03-job-b.mp3` (~22 s — pad with slower pace if needed)

```text
My antenna is my phone. Every number I measure goes to scientists in Houston. Your turn. What was the job that mattered most?
```

### `s3-story-04-bounce-a.mp3` (~30 s)

```text
Landing was supposed to be one soft touch. My radar would cut the engines at the right second. Bright rocks in the crater confused that radar. The engines stayed on. I bounced twice, then settled on a slope inside the crater. I was tipped, but my legs, camera, and scoop still worked. The soil test could go on.
```

### `s3-story-05-bounce-b.mp3` (~12 s — record with `s3-quiz-03-bounce.mp3` in one take OR slow down to ~25 s)

## Quiz — one file per chapter (~30 s each)

Read **in this order** inside the clip: question → hint → correct → wrong answer voice.

### `s3-quiz-01-meet.mp3`

```text
When did Surveyor 3 reach the Moon? Hmm, not that one. The astronauts of Apollo 12 arrived in November 1969. April 20, 1967. I waited more than two years so people could walk over and visit me. I got here first. April 1967 — Apollo 12 didn't arrive until November 1969.
```

### `s3-quiz-02-job.mp3`

```text
What was Surveyor 3's most important job? Hmm, not that one. Think about the footpads and the scoop, not a city. Yes. The soil was firm — about as strong as damp beach sand. A lunar lander could stand on it. My real job was the soil. If the dust was too soft, Apollo could not land.
```

### `s3-quiz-03-bounce.mp3`

```text
The landing radar glitches. What is the safe move? Hmm, not that one. Engines that stay on push you back off the ground. Cut the engines. I bounced twice because mine stayed lit. Then I could finally rest and dig. Leaving the engines on is what made me bounce. A pilot cuts them by hand.
```

### `s3-quiz-04-visit.mp3`

```text
How close did Apollo 12 land to Surveyor 3? Hmm, not that one. Conrad joked that Surveyor was right down the middle of the road. About 600 feet. Close enough to walk over, say hello, and carry pieces of me back to Earth. They found me easily. Intrepid landed about 600 feet away — a short Moon walk.
```


```text

Soil test complete. Firm as damp beach sand. Apollo 12 is cleared to land. Call them in. Soil is firm. Pictures are in. Open the dish and call Pete Conrad and Alan Bean — they are go. Dish open. Clear the landing for Intrepid.
```

```text
Houston, this is Surveyor. Pad is safe — Intrepid, you are go for landing! November 1969. Watch Intrepid ease down onto the pad my footpads already tested. You are looking out Intrepid's window. The engines are easing the lander onto soil my footpads already tested. Touchdown. Legs on the dust.
```
